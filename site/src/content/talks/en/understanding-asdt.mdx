    <p class="slide-kicker">06 / 12 · the memory problem</p>
    <h2>AI forgets. Long sessions don't just lose facts — they lose judgment.</h2>
    <p><strong>Law 1 in practice:</strong> every step starts with a clean, scoped context and hands off through a written artifact. The work lives in documents — not in a conversation that might not exist tomorrow.</p>
    <p><strong>Concrete loss:</strong> ask one long chat to "use the auth pattern we agreed on" and by message 200 it has quietly forgotten which pattern that was — and invents a new one. A written artifact never forgets.</p>
    <p>This deck? Worked example. Every specialist read one artifact, wrote one artifact, returned. No drift. No giant chat. Just documents that survive. 🐈</p>

  <div class="slide-content">
    <p class="slide-kicker">11 / 12 · the rules that hold it together</p>
    <h2>Three laws. Every part of ASDT traces back to one of them.</h2>
  </div>
  <figure class="slide-figure">
    <div class="slide-figure-inner">

        <div class="law-card law-card--l1">

            <span class="law-number">Law 1</span>
          </div>
          <h3 class="law-title">Artifacts over memory</h3>
          <p class="law-summary">The only thing that survives a context reset is what you wrote down.</p>
          <p class="law-body">Conversations evaporate; documents persist. Every step reads its inputs as written artifacts and leaves one written artifact behind, so the next step never depends on a chat that no longer exists.</p>
          <blockquote class="law-example"><span class="law-ex-label">In this deck</span> Building this deck: the discovery brief was written once and survived a cold chat — the PM read it days later with zero conversation history.</blockquote>
        </div>
        <div class="law-card law-card--l2">
          <div class="law-card-head">
            <span class="law-icon" aria-hidden="true">⊘</span>

          </div>
          <h3 class="law-title">Humans gate, AI executes</h3>
          <p class="law-summary">The machine never decides; it delivers options and waits.</p>
          <p class="law-body">Between specialists sits a checkpoint. The AI lays out trade-offs and stops. A human reviews, picks a direction, and only then does the next specialist proceed. Judgment stays human-owned.</p>
          <blockquote class="law-example"><span class="law-ex-label">In this deck</span> Building this deck: at the PM → Architect checkpoint the AI offered Markdown, a slide framework, or raw HTML. Lee chose the format — the AI did not.</blockquote>

          <p class="law-summary">Every specialist sees the problem through a different lens before a single line ships.</p>
          <p class="law-body">One model, many lenses. Research, requirements, architecture, and design each interrogate the work before implementation begins — catching the gaps a single agent, with a single bias, never would.</p>
          <blockquote class="law-example"><span class="law-ex-label">In this deck</span> Building this deck: seven specialists weighed in before a single slide was written — the angle, the structure, and the format were all settled first.</blockquote>
